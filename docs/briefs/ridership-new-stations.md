# Brief: Ridership forecasts for new or changed 'L' stations

Generated by `python -m simchi.briefs` from `reports/ridership_validation.json`. Gating test: ridership_rail, W1 re-baseline (pre-registered in simchi/eval/ridership.py) — passed.

## Finding

For 'L' stations without ridership history, the simulation calibrated with scheduled service predicts weekday ridership better than scheduled service alone: held-out mean absolute log error 0.517 vs 0.522 (123 'L' stations).

## Evidence

- Trained on 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31, tested on 2026-01-01 to 2026-08-31, both after CTA's 2025-01-01 counting-method change; 5 folds of units.
- Constant baseline: 0.678; simulation alone: 0.989.
- Across 5 synthetic populations the calibrated model's error ranges 0.515-0.523; it misses the schedule baseline in 2 of them.

## What we don't know

- For an 'L' station that has history, its own previous year predicts far better (error 0.064): this finding is only about new or changed 'L' stations.
- The model has commute demand only; non-commute travel enters through the calibration.
- The 2025-26 data were examined in an earlier design, so they are held out by design, not unseen.

## Cost

Not costed: no cost figure is in reports/.

## Pilot proposal

Before the next new or restructured 'L' station opens, publish the model's weekday ridership forecast for it, then compare with CTA's counts for its first 3 full months.

**Success criterion (fixed before the pilot):** The forecast's absolute log error for the new station is below that of the scheduled-service baseline fitted on the same 2025 data.
